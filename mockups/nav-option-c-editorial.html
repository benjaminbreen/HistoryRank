Option C — Editorial Underline

Design Approach

Key idea: Borrows from newspaper/journal mastheads. The title is large, serif, and typographically confident with a gold period accent. A thin rule separates the masthead from the underline tab bar, which scrolls horizontally on narrow screens. The active tab gets a gold underline — simple and unmistakable.

Improvements over current: The two-row split creates a clear hierarchy: brand identity above, navigation below. The "HR" icon is gone in favor of the wordmark+dot, which is more distinctive and editorial. About/Methodology/Caveats are promoted to small caps in the upper right, which actually makes them more discoverable than the current layout where they're mixed in with primary nav. The count badge on "Rankings" provides a live data signal.

Strengths

Most visually distinctive of the three. The editorial feel matches a project about historical scholarship. Horizontal scroll on the tab bar means it scales to any number of tabs. Feels authoritative and considered.

Trade-offs

Takes more vertical space (two rows). The wordmark without the "HR" icon loses some brand recognition at small sizes. The underline style is common enough that it won't feel novel.
